# Synthetic biomarker study: results

Biomarker results for a made-up study of 200 participants, twelve
biomarkers and five visits, with one row per participant, biomarker and
visit. It is the one table every biomarker chart needs, and it exists so
that a test can assert an answer that is known in advance.

## Usage

``` r
Synthetic_Results
```

## Format

A data frame with 11472 rows and 6 columns. The column names are the
defaults safety.viz uses for results data.

- USUBJID:

  `character` Participant identifier, `BIO-001` to `BIO-200`.

- VISIT:

  `character` Visit name: `Baseline`, `Week 2`, `Week 4`, `Week 8` or
  `Week 12`.

- VISITNUM:

  `integer` Visit order, the week number: 0, 2, 4, 8 or 12.

- TEST:

  `character` Biomarker name, in ASCII: `IL-6`, `CRP`, `TNF-alpha`,
  `IL-10`, `IFN-gamma`, `IL-2`, `IL-1beta`, `IL-8`, `VEGF`, `Ferritin`,
  `D-dimer` or `LDH`.

- STRESU:

  `character` Unit of the result.

- STRESN:

  `numeric` Result, to three decimal places; missing where a sample was
  not analysable.

## Source

Generated by `data-raw/synthetic-study.R`. The same table is in the
package as
`system.file("extdata", "synthetic_results.csv", package = "gsm.bio")`.

## Details

Everything in it is synthetic. The values are random draws from the
model in the script `data-raw/synthetic-study.R`, run with the seed
recorded in
[Synthetic_Truth](https://jwildfire.github.io/gsm.bio/reference/Synthetic_Truth.md).
No real study data was used and nothing was copied from any real study;
the biomarker names are generic analyte names, with levels chosen only
to look plausible.

## Planted effects

Two of the three planted effects are in this table. Their true sizes are
parameters of the generating model, held in
[Synthetic_Truth](https://jwildfire.github.io/gsm.bio/reference/Synthetic_Truth.md).

- **A difference between two groups in change from baseline.** In the
  Treatment arm, IL-6 is lower by 1.5 pg/mL at every visit after
  Baseline. So the difference between the arms in mean change from
  Baseline, Treatment minus Placebo, is -1.5 pg/mL at each of Week 2,
  Week 4, Week 8 and Week 12. The arms do not differ at Baseline.

- **A correlation between two biomarkers.** TNF-alpha and IL-10 are
  drawn from a bivariate normal distribution with a Pearson correlation
  of 0.6 at each visit, on the scale stored here.

The third, a survival difference between high and low Baseline CRP, is
in
[Synthetic_Outcomes](https://jwildfire.github.io/gsm.bio/reference/Synthetic_Outcomes.md).
Every other biomarker is null by construction: unrelated to arm, to
every other biomarker and to the outcome. Each planted biomarker carries
its one effect only.

The true sizes describe the model before the stored values were rounded
to three decimal places, which moves none of them by an amount a test
can see.

## Model

Each biomarker has a participant-level part, 60 percent of its variance,
and a visit-level part. IL-6, TNF-alpha, IL-10, IL-2, IL-1beta, VEGF and
LDH are normal on the stored scale; CRP, IFN-gamma, IL-8, Ferritin and
D-dimer are log-normal, so right-skewed.

## Imperfections

Baseline is complete: every participant has all twelve biomarkers. After
Baseline, two things are missing completely at random, as they would be
in a real study, so that a chart has dropped rows to count:

- a participant misses a whole visit with probability 0.05, and that
  visit has no rows at all;

- a result is not analysable with probability 0.02, and its row is
  present with a missing `STRESN`.
